# E2 — Stocks that drop out of the Russell 2000: does the forced selling bounce back?

**Verdict: DOES NOT HOLD, in both pre-registered cells.** Source: [`risultati/verdetto.md`](risultati/verdetto.md)
(generated by `analisi.py --verdetto`), confirmed after a post-verdict data check in
[`2026-09-22_addendum_verdetto.md`](2026-09-22_addendum_verdetto.md).

*Draft study page. The working documents in this folder are in Italian; this page is the English summary.*

## The claim being tested

Every June, FTSE Russell reconstitutes its US indexes. Small caps that fall out of the Russell 2000 on the way
**down** are sold by every fund that tracks the index, on a fixed date, regardless of price. The folk story is
simple: selling that is forced and price-insensitive pushes the stock below fair value, and once the index funds are
done the price recovers. Buy after the selling ends, hold a year, beat comparable companies.

Mechanism, as implied by the pre-registered question («if I enter when the forced selling is over»): temporary
**price pressure** from index-deletion selling, which should reverse once the selling is over. If that is real, the reversal should be visible against peers of the same size and past return, in most years,
and not in a placebo built from stocks that were not deleted.

## Design (pre-registered)

Written on 2026-09-16, before any excess return was computed or looked at
([`2026-09-16_preregistrazione.md`](2026-09-16_preregistrazione.md)); changes were dated addenda, never rewrites
([`2026-09-21_addendum_falsi_positivi.md`](2026-09-21_addendum_falsi_positivi.md),
[`2026-09-22_addendum_fermata1.md`](2026-09-22_addendum_fermata1.md)). Design decisions: ADR-038 … ADR-045
in [`DECISIONS.md`](../../DECISIONS.md).

- **Who is a case.** A stock held by IWM (Russell 2000 ETF) on 31 March and gone from both IWM and IWB (Russell 1000)
  on 30 June of the same year (30 September in 2019 and 2024), or down to at most 50% of its March shares (ADR-039). Index membership is rebuilt from
  **iShares Trust's own SEC filings** (N-Q, N-CSR, N-PORT), because historical holdings files are not available.
  2015-2025.
- **Identity check.** Name → CIK, then the implied price in the fund's position must match the raw close within 3%.
  Unverified identity = excluded, counted.
- **Definition checked against the official lists.** For every year checked against a public FTSE Russell deletion
  list (2016, 2017, 2021-2025) the definition finds 97.8%-100% of the official deletions, with 0.6%-8.1% false positives —
  under the 10% stop threshold set in advance ([`risultati/falsi_positivi_ricalcolati.md`](risultati/falsi_positivi_ricalcolati.md)).
- **Exclusions,** in order and counted: identity not verified; acquired, merged or delisted in the quarter (EDGAR
  filings: Form 25, 15-12, merger proxies, tender offers, 8-K item 1.03); insufficient prices; SPACs.
- **Quality filters,** point-in-time from XBRL (only facts filed before Rank Day): positive 12-month operating cash
  flow; net cash or net debt/EBITDA < 3; share count up at most 5% in 12 months; no announced merger.
- **Peers.** Five stocks that stayed in IWM, same tercile of 6-month return, nearest in size.
- **Entries.** A = reconstitution close; B1/B2/B3 = first day the selling looks over (no new lows for N sessions,
  volume back to normal), forced at a limit; C = reconstitution + 32. Tested: no future price changes an entry
  (360 checks).
- **Verdict cells, fixed in advance.** Q1 "investment": entry B2, 252 sessions. Q2 "short rebound": entry A,
  32 sessions (the pre-registration ties it to the December 2026 reconstitution). Everything else is descriptive.
- **Verdict rule, identical for both cells.** HOLDS only if all five hold: ≥ 100 cases in ≥ 9 years; median excess
  > 0; mean of yearly means > 0 with t ≥ 2 across years; mean > 0 in both 2015-2019 and 2020-2025; placebo with
  |t| < 2 and a lower mean. INCONCLUSIVE if median and mean of yearly means are positive but 1 ≤ t < 2 (or the sample
  is too small). Otherwise DOES NOT HOLD.
- **Placebos.** Q1: "false events" — the smallest stocks that stayed in the index, one per real case, same filters,
  same entry rule. Q2: the same window shifted 63 sessions later.
- **Statistics.** Yearly means first, then the t-statistic across years (degrees of freedom = years − 1), so one busy
  year cannot carry the result.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1. Investment | B2 × 252 | 225 | 11 | +4.8% | +1.6% | 0.24 (10) | +0.3% | +2.7% | +5.6%, t 1.03 | **DOES NOT HOLD** |
| 2. Short rebound | A × 32 | 234 | 11 | −0.6% | +1.5% | 0.45 (10) | −3.6% | +5.7% | −0.5%, t −0.15 | **DOES NOT HOLD** |

- **Investment:** the median is positive, but the yearly means swing from −45% to +30% and the t across years is
  0.24. The decisive failure is the placebo: stocks that were **not** deleted, bought with the same rule, did better
  (+5.6%) than the deleted ones (+1.6%). Whatever drives the number, it is not the deletion.
- **Short rebound:** negative median, negative in 2015-2019, t 0.45. There is no reliable bounce in the six weeks
  after reconstitution.
- **Descriptive cells** (19 of them, `risultati/verdetto.md`): none clears t ≥ 2 on the cell that was pre-registered;
  a few other entry/horizon combinations touch t ≈ 2.1-2.3, which is what you expect to find by looking at 19 cells.
  They decide nothing.

## Why it did not work

1. **The sample that survives honest filters is small and unhealthy.** Of 1,346 downward exits that reach the quality
   filters, 567 have positive operating cash flow, 632 do not and 147 cannot be checked
   ([`risultati/conteggi.md`](risultati/conteggi.md)). Small caps leaving the
   Russell 2000 burn cash about half the time; the "good company, temporarily mispriced" case is a minority.
2. **Year effects dominate.** With 5 to 64 cases per year, one bad year (2023: −45%, 9 cases) or one good year moves
   the average more than any deletion effect could. Pooling all cases would have hidden this; the t across years
   exposes it.
3. **The placebo beats the treatment.** The smallest stocks that stayed in the index, bought with the same rule, did
   better (+5.6%) than the deleted ones (+1.6%). Whatever moves small stocks after reconstitution, being deleted adds
   nothing to it.
4. **Data traps that would have inflated the result were closed before looking.** Delisted names are kept (last
   price, or the cash-offer price when the deal document is in the cache); ticker changes were later found to be
   treated as delistings, and following the company to its new code moves the investment cell from +1.63% to +1.74%,
   t 0.24 → 0.25, computed for the 4 of 6 such securities where it can be (`2026-09-22_addendum_verdetto.md`). The verdict does not change.

## Reproducing

- The **code** is here: `analisi.py` (verdict), `uscite.py`, `identita.py`, `filtri_xbrl.py`, `ingressi.py`,
  `rendimenti.py`, `partecipazioni_sec.py` (index membership from SEC filings), `falsi_positivi.py` (check against the
  official lists).
- The **verdict cannot be regenerated from this repository**: prices come from a licensed end-of-day archive (EODHD,
  personal-use licence, via `market_data.api`), which is not redistributable. The referto and the aggregate JSON are
  kept as documents. Per-security files (case lists with market caps and 6-month returns, per-name false-positive
  tables) were removed from the public copy; the aggregate tables are unchanged.
- **Tests** run on synthetic data only: `python backtest/russell_exits/test_rendimenti.py` (and `test_filtri.py`,
  `test_ingressi.py`, `test_serie_eodhd.py`, `test_uscite.py`), or all study suites with `bash backtest/test_studi.sh`.
