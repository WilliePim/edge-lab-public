# US insider buying in $50-300M stocks: an effect found, then not confirmed

**Verdict of the pre-registered re-test: INCONCLUSIVE — R1** ("placebo not ≈ 0, no matching fixes it").
Source: [`step2/report.md`](step2/report.md), generated by `step2_analysis.py`. The secondary criterion copied from
the Swedish test is INCONCLUSIVE too (coverage 54.4% < 60%). The Swedish replication itself lives in a separate
repository, <https://github.com/WilliePim/fi-insider-scanner>, and **does not hold** there.

*Draft study page. The working documents are in Italian; this page is the English summary of one story in three
steps.*

## The claim and its mechanism

The claim: when insiders buy their own company's stock on the open market, the stock subsequently beats comparable
stocks. The mechanism behind it is **information**: insiders know more about the business than the market, and a
purchase with their own money reveals it (the pre-registration takes this premise for granted rather than spelling it
out, e.g. §5 on why the peers' quiet window must not look ahead). If that is the mechanism, the excess return must
survive a comparison with peers of the same size and past return, and must not appear before the purchase.

## 1. The discovery (2026-09-01): a number that looks like an edge

A descriptive event-time backtest over the whole Form 4 corpus (open-market purchases, code P, ≥ $25k per line,
2015-2026) split by market cap. In the $50-300M band, one event per issuer every 126 days, 126 trading sessions after
the filing, against IWM:

> n 3,213, mean **+3.50%**, median −1.94%, **t 4.30** (iid), bootstrap CI [+1.95%, +5.19%]
> — [`reports/backtest-event-time-2026-09-01-dilution-50-300M.md`](../../reports/backtest-event-time-2026-09-01-dilution-50-300M.md)

The population in that report excludes filings flagged by a **dilution predicate** (`dilution.jsonl`, the "gate" in
the report names). That predicate is part of the event definition: it was **defined for these reports and for the
re-test, and it has not been validated** as a filter. Nothing here shows that it works.

The other bands and the unfiltered version are in the same folder
(`reports/backtest-event-time-2026-09-01*.md`). Nothing about this was pre-registered, and t 4.30 is an iid t on
overlapping, clustered events.

## 2. The first check (same day): a matched control shrinks it

[`reports/matched-control-2026-09-01.md`](../../reports/matched-control-2026-09-01.md) pairs each event with a stock
of the same size band, same date, no insider buying in the previous 60 days. In the $50-300M band at 126 days:

> n 3,009, **+2.41%, t 2.02**, CI [+0.04%, +4.58%]

and, split by past 12-month return, the worst tercile carries it (**+6.49%, t 2.79**), the middle one is negative
(−1.70%). That is the signature of **reversal**: insiders buy after a stock has fallen, and fallen stocks bounce. The
insider may be a marker, not a cause. A calendar-time replication of the classic opportunistic/routine split
(Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski 2012) did not replicate either: [`reports/cmp_calendar_time_long.md`](../../reports/cmp_calendar_time_long.md).

## 3. The pre-registered re-test (2026-09-15)

Written before any post-event return was computed: [`../2026-09-15_preregistration.md`](../2026-09-15_preregistration.md)
and addenda 1-3 (population, calendar, survival), 4-5 (split corrections, post hoc). Decisions ADR-001 … ADR-035
and ADR-037 in [`DECISIONS.md`](../../DECISIONS.md).

- **Step 1 — reproduce the original, character by character.** Done: same n, mean, median and t
  ([`step1/report.md`](step1/report.md)). Clustered by issuer (CR1), the original's t stays 4.30 for variant (b).
- **Population fixed in advance:** one event per issuer per 12-month window; peers drawn from issuers with XBRL shares
  and prices; delistings kept (last close held flat), with survival scenarios for events whose outcome is unobserved.
- **Two matchings, always both:** size only (**M_size**) and size + 12-month momentum (**M_mom**), so the reversal
  story from step 2 is controlled rather than argued about.
- **Two placebos:** the same event-minus-peer difference measured on windows **before** the event —
  P1 = (−252, −126] sessions, P2 = (−504, −378]. If the matched peer is really comparable, the difference before the
  event should be ≈ 0.
- **Verdict table fixed in advance (R0′-R6, addendum 1).** HOLDS only if the survival scenarios do not decide the
  result (R0′), and the effect survives (mean > 0, CR1 confidence interval excluding zero) both against the reference
  matching, chosen among those whose placebos are ≈ 0, and against M_mom, whose own placebo must be ≈ 0 too. A placebo
  is ≈ 0 when its CR1 interval includes zero and its mean is within ±1.5 points. If no matching has credible
  placebos, the result cannot be read (R1, INCONCLUSIVE).
- **Power, computed in advance:** on the primary population the minimum detectable effect against peers is
  3.84-4.24% (addendum 1 §7), **above** the original +3.50%. The addendum says so in advance: the test has less than
  80% power for an effect of that size, and an INCONCLUSIVE outcome is expected even if +3.5% were true.

| vs IWM | 2,324 | +3.05% | 3.22 | +1.19% … +4.92% |
| vs size peers (M_size) | 2,319 | +1.83% | 1.27 | −1.01% … +4.67% |
| vs size + momentum peers (M_mom) | 2,166 | +1.92% | 1.35 | −0.88% … +4.73% |

| placebo (before the event) | n | mean | t CR1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| M_size P1 | 2,129 | −0.51% | −0.37 |
| **M_size P2** | 1,778 | **+4.53%** | 2.92 |
| M_mom P1 (compressed by construction) | 2,159 | +4.12% | 4.11 |
| **M_mom P2** | 1,817 | **+3.31%** | 2.33 |

- Against the index the effect is still there (+3.05%, t 3.22). **Against comparable companies it is not
  distinguishable from zero** (+1.8% to +1.9%, t 1.3).
- And the comparison itself is not clean: one and a half to two years **before** the insider buys, the event stocks already
  beat their matched peers by 3-4.5% (P2), under both matchings. The peers are not comparable in some way neither
  size nor momentum captures, so even the +1.8% cannot be read. That is row R1: **INCONCLUSIVE**.
- Decomposition: 39-40% of the excess over IWM is the peers' own excess over IWM — part of the original +3.50% was
  simply "small stocks vs a small-cap index".
- Descriptive cells: > $300M shows +3.37% vs size peers (t 5.25), a band outside the pre-registered question;
  the $50-300M sub-period 2022-2025 against M_mom is +5.95% (t 2.69) — one sub-period out of many looks, decided
  nothing. **Closed period** (buys within 10 sessions of a 10-Q/10-K vs. outside): neither side is distinguishable
  from zero against peers (+2.10%, t 0.85 inside; +1.61%, t 0.96 outside), and the comparison has no power below 5%
  ([`step4/report.md`](step4/report.md)).
- **Survival:** coverage 63.4% of the cell; 38 events terminated in the window were classified from EDGAR filings
  (acquisition 32, bankruptcy 3, voluntary delisting 2, unresolved 1) with a classifier validated at 90% on 40 hand
  labels (36/40, exactly at the threshold). *Public copy: one of the 40 hand-labelled documents (a concordant
  case) is not included, so the 39 published labels give 35/39 = 89.7%; the registered verdict on all 40 stands
  ([`step2_prep/validazione.md`](step2_prep/validazione.md)).*
- **Post-hoc split corrections** (addenda 4-5): market cap = as-reported XBRL shares × split-adjusted price is wrong
  by the split factor when a split happens later. Two corrections were tried and **both failed their own validation
  gate** — the XBRL split detector agreed on 33/40 (82.5% < 90%; three of the 40 excerpts are not in the public copy, and the
  37 published give 32/37 = 86.5%, still below), Yahoo's split table was confirmed by insider prices
  on 590/686 splits (86.0% < 90%, [`../splits_validazione.md`](../splits_validazione.md)). So no corrected numbers
  were computed, as agreed in advance. With that, the insider line of research was closed.

## Why it did not work

1. **The benchmark was doing the work.** "Beats IWM" mixes the insider signal with "small, recently fallen stocks vs a
   small-cap index". Against peers of the same size the effect roughly halves and loses significance.
2. **Reversal is the rival explanation, and it fits.** The effect concentrates in stocks that had fallen most.
   Insiders buy after falls; falls mean-revert.
3. **The placebo is the honest test, and it failed.** A matched peer that already differs from the event stock
   18-24 months earlier is not a control. When no matching passes the placebo, the only defensible verdict is "cannot
   be read", not a positive number with an asterisk.
4. **iid t-stats on clustered events overstate certainty.** 3,213 events come from ~1,100 issuers; the original t
   4.30 was never the right yardstick for an issuer-level effect.
5. **Replication elsewhere failed.** The same idea tested on Swedish insider filings does not hold:
   <https://github.com/WilliePim/fi-insider-scanner>.

An INCONCLUSIVE verdict is not proof that the effect is zero: the test was underpowered even for +3.5%, and blind to
+1-2%.
What it does show is that the published-looking +3.50% (t 4.30) was not the insider effect it seemed to be.

## Reproducing

- **Code:** `step1_analysis.py` (replication), `survival.py` (population and survival), `step2_analysis.py` (matching,
  placebos, verdict), `step4_closed_period.py`, `split_validazione.py` and `../splits.py` (split corrections),
  `diagnostica_1504/` (post-hoc diagnostics). Statistics come from `tools/backtest_event_time.py`,
  `tools/matched_control.py`, `tools/table3_regression.py`.
- **Data not in this repository:** the Form 4 corpus is rebuilt from the SEC bulk insider datasets (public), XBRL
  from EDGAR (public), but the price panel (Yahoo, per-ticker series) and the derived market caps are not
  redistributed, so the reports cannot be regenerated from here. Per-issuer lists (terminated events with returns,
  per-ticker split validation rows) were reduced to counts in the public copy; third-party contact details in quoted
  8-K excerpts were removed.
- **Tests on synthetic data** (tickers AAA/BBB/CCC, invented prices, no network):
  `python backtest/rematch_50_300m/test_sintetico.py` checks the CR1 formula against the pre-registration, the verdict
  table R0′-R6 (including the R1 path the real test took), the survival classifier and the common-calendar excess;
  `python backtest/test_splits_sintetico.py` checks the split rules. All study suites: `bash backtest/test_studi.sh`.
