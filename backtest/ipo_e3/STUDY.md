# E3 — Broken IPOs and strong IPOs (US, 2012-2024)

**Verdicts: broken IPOs INCONCLUSIVE (the population is too rare to test); strong IPOs DO NOT HOLD (t 1.07).**
Source: [`risultati/referto.md`](risultati/referto.md), generated by `analisi.py` and `referto.py`.

*Draft study page. The working documents in this folder are in Italian; this page is the English summary.*

## The two claims being tested

Two opposite stories are told about newly listed stocks, and this study tested both with the same machinery:

1. **Broken IPOs recover.** A *healthy* company whose stock is crushed below its offer price by technical selling
   (flippers, lock-up expiry) should recover against comparable companies once the selling is over. Buy when the
   selling ends, hold a year.
2. **Strong IPOs keep winning.** An IPO that never closed below its offer price up to the lock-up expiry should beat
   comparable companies over the following year.

Declared mechanism: for broken IPOs it is in the question itself — **technical selling** pushes a healthy company
below its offer price, and the price should recover once that selling is over, which is why the health filters and
the "selling is over" entry exist. For strong IPOs the pre-registration states the question and no mechanism. Either
way, a real effect should show against comparable companies, in most cohort years, and not in a placebo window.

They were designed as opposites on purpose: if both "held", something would be wrong with the design, and the
referto would have had to discuss that before any conclusion.

## Design (pre-registered)

Written on 2026-09-22 before any excess return was computed
([`2026-09-22_preregistrazione.md`](2026-09-22_preregistrazione.md)); the user's decisions at the first stop are in
[`2026-09-22_addendum_fermata1.md`](2026-09-22_addendum_fermata1.md), also written before any return. Design
decisions: ADR-046 … ADR-052 in [`DECISIONS.md`](../../DECISIONS.md).

- **Universe.** Every 424B4/424B1 prospectus on EDGAR 2012-2024 (424B3 only as a fallback), 5,285 issuers. Excluded
  without reading text: classes already listed, SPACs, REITs, banks, closed-end funds, foreign issuers/ADRs,
  co-registrants. Excluded from the prospectus text: SPAC trust accounts, units, declared REITs, no offer price,
  price under $5, LP/LLC "common units".
- **Prospectus fields** (offer price, date, lock-up length, shares) are parsed from the filing and **hand-checked on
  30 random prospectuses**: 100% on price, 92-97% on the other fields ([`verifica_30.md`](verifica_30.md)).
- **Dates.** Lock-up expiry S = offer date + declared lock-up; control date = S + 10 sessions.
- **Groups.** *Broken*: close at the control date ≤ −30% from the offer price. *Strong*: never closed below the offer
  price from the first bar to the control date. Intermediate: counted only. Offer price restated in the series' units
  only for splits after the first bar (ADR-052); a first close outside 0.5-3× the offer price means the series is not
  the IPO's and the case is excluded (ADR-051).
- **Health filters for broken IPOs,** point-in-time at entry: positive operating cash flow (12 months, or the latest
  6-12-month year-to-date figure where 12 months do not exist yet); net cash or net debt/EBITDA < 3; no new issuance
  since the IPO; no announced merger.
- **Peers.** Five listed US common stocks, same tercile of 6-month return, nearest in market cap, listed for at least
  three years and passing the same cash-flow and debt filters; banks, REITs and SPACs excluded as in the universe.
- **Entries.** Broken: A (day after expiry), **B** (first close with no new low for 20 sessions and normal volume,
  earliest S + 30, forced at S + 126), C (S + 63). Strong: the control date.
- **Verdict cells.** Broken: **B × 252 sessions**. Strong: **entry × 252 sessions**. All other cells descriptive.
- **Verdict rule** (same for both): HOLDS only if ≥ 80 cases in ≥ 8 cohort years; median > 0; mean of yearly means
  > 0 with t ≥ 2; mean > 0 in 2012-2018 and 2019-2024; placebo (same window shifted 126 sessions later) with |t| < 2
  and a lower mean. INCONCLUSIVE if median and mean of yearly means are positive with 1 ≤ t < 2, **or** if the cell is
  below the size criterion, whatever the sign (the reading fixed in the first-stop addendum, before any return).
  Otherwise DOES NOT HOLD.
- **Delistings** are not dropped: last real price, or the cash deal price from the merger document; tickers are
  followed across renames and over-the-counter continuations.

## Result (aggregates from `risultati/referto.md`)

| question | cell | cases | cohort years | median | mean of yearly means | t (df) | 2012-18 | 2019-24 | placebo mean, t | verdict |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1. Broken IPOs | B × 252 | 17 | 7 | −22.0% | −4.8% | −0.42 (6) | −10.4% | +9.2% | −6.6%, t −0.53 | **INCONCLUSIVE** |
| 2. Strong IPOs | strong × 252 | 331 | 13 | −9.5% | +12.4% | 1.07 (12) | +13.8% | +10.7% | −0.9%, t −0.09 | **DOES NOT HOLD** |

- **Broken IPOs:** 195 IPOs were broken at the control date, but only **17** passed the health filters, mostly
  because only 41 of 194 had positive operating cash flow at entry. With 17 cases in 7 years the rule says
  INCONCLUSIVE whatever the sign — and the sign is not encouraging: median −22% against peers, 5 of 17 positive.
- **Strong IPOs:** for the typical strong IPO the answer is no — median −9.5% against peers, only 42% beat them. The
  positive mean of yearly means (+12.4%) is carried by a handful of very large winners and swings from year to year
  (t 1.07). The 2020-2021 cohort of strong IPOs lost about 40% against peers.
- **Context:** the average IPO underperforms comparable companies in the year after lock-up (base cohort median
  −17%). Strong IPOs do less badly, but they do not beat comparable companies.
- **Insider split (descriptive, expectation written in advance):** among the 17 broken cases, those with officer or
  director sales between expiry and entry did *better* (median +2.5%, 8 cases) than those without (−27.9%, 9 cases) —
  the opposite of the recorded expectation, on numbers too small to mean anything.

## Why it did not work

1. **"Healthy and broken" is almost an empty set.** An IPO that falls 30% below its offer price and still generates
   cash is rare. The recovery story needs a population that barely exists; the honest outcome is "cannot be tested",
   not "works".
2. **Strong IPOs are a right-skewed lottery.** The mean is positive only because of a few enormous winners; the median
   stock loses to its peers. A rule that buys all of them inherits the median, not the anecdote.
3. **The cohort matters more than the signal.** 2020-2021 strong IPOs lost ~40% against peers; any backtest that
   starts after 2021 or stops before 2020 tells a different story. Yearly means and a t across cohorts make that
   visible.
4. **Declared limits** (referto §I): 204 IPOs are excluded because the price archive flags an unverified split-like
   jump and drops their history up to it; on raw closes 58 of them would have been broken, probably the worst ones. Over half
   of broken IPOs enter at the first possible day or at the forced limit, so the "selling is over" rule often does
   not fire. For 11 IPOs the XBRL market cap is less than a tenth of the prospectus figure; removing them changes no verdict.

## Reproducing

- The **code** is here: `universo.py`, `bulk.py`, `prospetto.py`, `leggi_prospetti.py`, `casi.py` (groups and
  entries), `filtri.py`, `peer.py`, `analisi.py` (statistics and verdict), `referto.py`.
- The **verdict cannot be regenerated from this repository**: prices come from a licensed end-of-day archive (EODHD,
  personal-use licence, via `market_data.api`), which is not redistributable; prospectuses and XBRL come from EDGAR and
  can be re-downloaded (2,699 calls in the original run, capped at 3,000). Per-security tables (the 17 broken cases
  with names and returns, the per-name exclusion lists, the worked examples with peers' market caps) were removed from
  the public copy; the aggregate tables are unchanged.
- **Tests** on synthetic IPOs (tickers AAA … EEE, invented prices and CIKs):
  `python backtest/ipo_e3/test_sintetico.py`, or all study suites with `bash backtest/test_studi.sh`.
